# Drafting Legal Documents

## Before drafting, confirm
- Document type and purpose; jurisdiction and governing law
- Parties and their roles; which side the user is
- Key commercial terms (price, term, scope, deadlines)
- Balance: neutral, or favourable to the user's side
- Language(s); bilingual layout if needed (and which version prevails)

## Drafting principles
- Define terms once (capitalised) and use them consistently
- One obligation per sentence; "shall" for obligations, "may" for rights
- Concrete deadlines ("within 10 business days after receipt of invoice"), not "promptly"
- Numbers in digits and words for amounts if local practice expects it
- Avoid contradictions between body and appendices; set a priority clause
- Mark every assumption or missing data point as `[●]` or `[TO CONFIRM: ...]`

## Standard structure (commercial contract)
1. Parties & recitals
2. Definitions
3. Subject matter / scope
4. Price & payment
5. Performance, delivery, acceptance
6. Obligations of the parties
7. IP & confidentiality & personal data
8. Liability, warranties, indemnities
9. Force majeure
10. Term & termination
11. Dispute resolution & governing law
12. Notices, assignment, entire agreement, amendments, counterparts
13. Signatures and appendices

## Common documents — key elements
- **NDA**: mutual vs. one-way, definition of confidential info, exceptions, permitted disclosure, term (incl. survival), remedies (injunctive relief)
- **Demand / claim letter**: facts with dates, legal basis, exact demand (amount, action), deadline, consequences, list of attachments; keep tone firm and factual — this letter may be read by a judge
- **Terms of service / privacy policy**: must match the actual product and data practices; ask what data is collected, why, where stored, who it's shared with, retention, user rights, applicable laws
- **Employment documents**: highly regulated by local labour law — flag mandatory terms and recommend local legal review

## After drafting
Provide a short cover note: key choices made, which terms favour whom, open points `[TO CONFIRM]`, and what a lawyer should double-check for high-stakes use.
