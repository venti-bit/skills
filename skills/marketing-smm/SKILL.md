# Marketing & SMM

Produce marketing work that is specific to the brand, the audience, and the channel. Generic copy that could belong to any company is the main failure mode — avoid it.

## Step 1: Collect the brief (briefly)

Before writing, make sure you know these. Infer what you can from context and files; ask only about the gaps that would materially change the output (max 3 questions at once). If the user wants speed, proceed with stated assumptions.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Product / offer | What exactly is being sold or promoted, price, key differentiator |
| Audience | Who, their pain, their level of awareness (unaware → most aware) |
| Goal | Reach, engagement, leads, sales, retention — determines the CTA and metric |
| Channel(s) | Each platform has different format and length norms |
| Brand voice | Formal / friendly / bold / expert; words to use and avoid |
| Constraints | Budget, deadlines, legal limits (e.g. ad labelling laws, medical/financial claims) |
| Language & market | Write natively for the target market, not translated-sounding copy |

## Step 3: Produce the work

General rules for all output:

- **Lead with the hook.** The first line decides whether anything else is read. Offer 2-3 hook variants for important pieces.
- **One message, one CTA** per piece of content.
- **Benefits over features**, backed by concrete proof (numbers, cases, testimonials) — never invent proof. If proof is missing, put a clearly marked placeholder like `[client result: ___]`.
- **Native format** for each platform (length, hashtags, emoji density, line breaks, aspect ratio for visuals).
- **Variants**: for ads and headlines give 3-5 options that differ in angle (pain, gain, curiosity, social proof, urgency), not just wording.
- **Visual direction**: for visual platforms add a short brief for the designer (composition, text on image, format).

### Content plan format

When asked for a content plan, return a table:

| Date | Platform | Format | Rubric / pillar | Topic | Hook | CTA | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Balance content pillars (typical mix: ~40% useful/educational, ~25% engagement/community, ~20% social proof/behind the scenes, ~15% direct selling) and adjust to the goal. Offer to export as .xlsx if a spreadsheet skill is available.

### Competitor / positioning analysis

Structure: competitors' offers and prices → their messaging and channels → gaps → recommended positioning statement:
`For [audience] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative], because [reason to believe].`
State clearly which facts came from the user / sources and which are assumptions.

## Step 4: Self-check before delivering

- Would this copy still make sense with a competitor's name swapped in? If yes, make it more specific.
- Is there any fabricated statistic, review, or guarantee? Remove or mark as placeholder.
- Does it comply with platform rules and local advertising law (ad disclosure, health/finance claims, comparative advertising)? Flag risks.
- Is the length right for the channel?

## Output

Deliver ready-to-paste text first, then (briefly) the rationale and suggested metrics to watch. Don't pad with marketing theory the user didn't ask for.
